# Design System

## Design Direction

Inkwell is a nocturnal editorial studio: graphite workspace chrome frames a warm manuscript surface, so the writer can distinguish durable story truth from provisional AI output at a glance. The visual language is restrained, tactile, and operational rather than decorative.

## Mode

Operate. The interface supports long drafting sessions, frequent scanning, and high-consequence editorial decisions.

## Material and Color

- Graphite and deep ink surfaces establish the application shell.
- A warm paper surface is reserved for manuscript and draft work.
- Violet-blue is the singular action accent.
- Amber communicates a warning, rose communicates a blocking issue, and green communicates a saved or accepted state.
- Borders are quiet separators; elevation is reserved for overlays and floating tools.

## Typography

- UI uses a compact sans-serif stack for scanning and controls.
- Draft prose uses a high-legibility serif stack with a generous line height and a readable measure.
- Hierarchy comes from type scale, whitespace, and surface contrast rather than decorative labels or repeated cards.

## Layout

- Desktop uses a three-zone studio: navigation, primary writing surface, and contextual inspector.
- The writing surface is the visual anchor. Supporting data becomes lists, rails, and drawers instead of a dashboard mosaic.
- Mobile collapses secondary context into a drawer and keeps the primary action within reach.

## Interaction

- Generation exposes ordered stages, elapsed time, and recovery actions.
- Inspector panels slide or fade in with purpose; no ornamental motion.
- Draft acceptance and canon proposals make state transitions explicit through copy and semantic color.

## Accessibility Rules

- Every interactive control has a visible focus treatment and text or an accessible label.
- Status changes use an aria-live region.
- Modals and drawers protect focus and close with Escape.
- Reduced-motion users see immediate state changes without losing context.
